Cost of arrival, reads like this

4 points
01

FOB and arrival cost are different

FOB is the price up to the moment of loading on the ship. International transportation, insurance, tariffs, value-added tax, customs clearance, and inland transportation must be added to this to become the actual cost reaching the warehouse. When comparing only unit prices, it is common for the rankings to be reversed based on domestic arrival.

02

Tariff is attached to CIF

The taxable value is CIF, which is the product price plus international shipping charges and insurance premiums. If you declare the freight rate low, your taxes will be reduced, but if it is different from the actual fare, there is a risk of fines and additional collection. Enter the freight amount as the actual payment amount.

03

VAT is added on top of tariffs

Value added tax is 10% of (duty price + tariff). If customs duties are added, the base amount of value-added tax also increases. So, the effective tax burden for an item with a tariff of 8% is approximately 18.8%.

04

Small amount of tax exemption is based on the price of the product

Tax exemption for overseas direct purchases is determined based on the product price minus the international shipping cost (taxes and inland freight within the sending country are included). If the limit is exceeded, international shipping costs and insurance premiums will also be included in the taxable price. This is why the difference between just below and just above the limit is large.

FAQ

6 questions
What costs should be included in B2B calculations?
The cost of goods quoted by the supplier (FOB), international shipping charges, insurance premiums, tariff rates, customs clearance fees, and domestic inland transportation. This calculator automatically adds VAT. Items subject to individual consumption tax, liquor tax, and rural special tax (alcohol, cigarettes, automobiles, etc.) are subject to additional tax rates, so the results may differ from the actual results.
What exactly is 150 dollars for listing clearance and 200 US dollars?
If the goods for self-use are less than USD 150 (USD 200 for goods sent from the United States), they are cleared through list customs clearance without import declaration and are exempt from customs duties and value-added tax. Health functional foods, medicines, food, alcohol, cigarettes, functional cosmetics, agricultural and marine products subject to quarantine, wireless devices, etc. are excluded from customs clearance and are subject to import declaration regardless of the amount, and the duty-free limit at this time is $150 regardless of country.
Is it necessary to enter the personal customs clearance code?
On this page, it is optional. In reality, it is often necessary for express delivery list customs clearance, so its use has been mandatory since December 2020, and a one-year validity period will apply from 2026. The entered code is only checked for format on the screen and is not sent or saved to the server.
If I upload a photo, where does it go?
It's not going anywhere. It is only shown as a preview in the browser, and disappears when you reload the page. It is not stored on the server or database.
Where can I check the tariff rate?
Check with the HS code in the World HS → Tariff Table on the Customs Regulations Information Portal (UNI-PASS CLIP). Even for the same item, the basic tax rate, WTO agreement tax rate, and FTA agreement tax rate are different, so the actual applied tax rate must be entered for the calculation to be correct. If item classification is unclear, use a customs broker or item classification prior review.
Do used and limited editions also pay taxes?
If it is an imported product, it is subject to second-hand customs and value-added tax. However, if it is for personal use and a small amount, it can be exempt from tax, but if it is for sale or business, it is taxable regardless of the amount. If an overseas platform handles shipping and customs clearance and collects tax in advance, it may already be included in the invoice, so don't count it twice.

Calculation basis and privacy

Scope — This is a reference calculator that adds customs duties, VAT, and incidental costs based on the entered amount and exchange rate. The actual tax amount will vary depending on the tax exchange rate on the import declaration date, confirmed HS code, application of FTA, and customs judgment. Individual consumption tax, liquor tax, special tax for rural areas, and inspection and quarantine costs are not included.

Privacy — The entered item, amount, exchange rate, city, province, and personal customs clearance code are used for calculation only within the browser and are not transmitted or stored on the server. This calculator is a reference tool to help compare general import costs and is not a substitute for legal or commercial judgment regarding tariffs, customs clearance, or contracts. The example amounts shown are not related to actual market prices.
