How to read the numbers

4 points
01

Settlement and net profit differ

The settlement is what the platform pays into your account after taking its fee. Net profit is what is left once the purchase price, shipping, duty and VAT and packing come off that. A large settlement can still leave a negative net.

02

Return is measured against cost

Return on cost (ROI) here is net profit ÷ total cost. It is not the same number as margin on the sale price, so keep them apart. Put in ₩1m and keep ₩200,000 and the ROI is 20%, while against a ₩1.2m sale price it is 16.7%.

03

Break-even purchase price

Above this price you cannot make money even after selling. Use it as your line when negotiating or bidding. Where duty applies the tax climbs with the price too, so break-even jumps sharply around the exemption limit.

04

Duty and VAT sit on the customs value

Duty is charged on the goods value plus international freight (the customs value), and 10% VAT is then charged on the customs value plus the duty. An 8% duty works out to an 18.8% effective burden, and 13% to 24.3%.

FAQ

8 answers
When do duty and VAT apply?
Personal-use goods up to US$150 (US$200 for shipments from the United States) clear under list clearance with no duty or VAT. Above that, duty is charged on the goods value plus international freight, and 10% VAT is added on top. Items that cannot use list clearance — health supplements, some cosmetics, food, alcohol, tobacco and similar — need an import declaration whatever the amount.
I'm buying to resell — can I still use the US$150 exemption?
The list-clearance exemption assumes the goods are for personal use. Goods bought to sell can need an import declaration and be taxed even at a small amount, so it is safer to tick “for resale” here and include the tax. The actual decision rests with customs; a registered business can often deduct import VAT as input tax.
Why is duty on shoes and clothing 13%?
Korea's basic rate is 13% for most shoes and clothing. Bags, wallets, electronics, watches and sports goods are usually 8%. An FTA rate can bring it to 0% depending on origin, but individual direct purchases are normally assessed at the basic rate. Check the exact rate by HS code on the Korea Customs tariff portal.
How is the KREAM fee calculated?
KREAM charges a basic fee plus a level fee. Since 2 March 2026, for general categories the level fee is 5.5–6.0% (VAT excluded) and the basic fee is ₩2,500 (VAT excluded). A first-time Level 1 seller should budget 6.6% + ₩2,750 with VAT. Those are this calculator's defaults; lower the rate as your level rises. The fee cap (₩300,000 including VAT) is not applied here.
How do Bunjang and Danggeun fees differ?
At Bunjang's standard shop the seller pays 6% of the item price for payments from 17 September 2025 (buyers pay nothing). Danggeun charges sellers no fee; with its protected payment or buy-now the buyer pays 3.3%. Cash deals in person are free for both sides. Pro shops and category rates differ, so check the estimated payout in the seller centre as well.
SoldOut isn't in the list.
SoldOut has announced it will close on 31 December 2026, with general trading ending first on 1 December 2026. It is therefore not a preset here; Musinsa Used stands in as the consignment route for fashion and second-hand. Musinsa Used takes about 15% commission plus a ₩7,000 merchandising fee, so put 7,000 in the fixed-fee box to compare.
What is the difference between return on cost (ROI) and margin?
Return on cost (ROI) measures what you earned against the money you put in: net profit ÷ total cost. Margin is what is left against the sale price: net profit ÷ sale price. Because the sale price is larger than the cost, margin is the smaller of the two. The line under the calculator shows both numbers.
Does registering as a business change the tax?
For a VAT-registered business, the VAT paid on import can usually be deducted as input tax, so the real cost falls. On the other side, profit is subject to VAT and income tax (or corporate tax). This calculator shows the cash flow of one deal only, so check business accounting with your tax adviser.
